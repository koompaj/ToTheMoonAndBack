### Notes
1. **Scope and Coverage:** 22 peer-reviewed and NASA primary-source items covering all 18 requested flight experiments: BASS, BASS-II, Saffire-I, Saffire-II, Saffire-III, SAME, SAME-R, FLEX, FLEX-2, CFI, SPICE, SLICE, ACME BRE, ACME CFI-G, ACME Flame Design, ACME CLD Flame, ACME E-FIELD Flames, and ACME s-Flame.
2. **Key Fire-Safety Takeaways Across Themes:**
   - *Flammability & Ventilation Hazards (BASS, BASS-II, ACME BRE):* In low-speed flows (< 1 cm/s), stable dim blue flames can persist undetected and flare up unpredictably with ventilation changes. In elevated-oxygen atmospheres, solid materials (PMMA, Nylon) can sustain burning without ventilation airflow, directly challenging the standard emergency protocol of turning off cabin circulation to extinguish fires.
   - *Scale Effects (Saffire-I, II, III):* Large-scale solid flame spread in microgravity reaches a limiting flame length and steady spread rate rather than continuously accelerating as upward fires do on Earth.
   - *Smoke Detection (SAME, SAME-R):* Overheated electronic insulation (Teflon, Kapton) produces ultra-fine sub-micron aerosols that are challenging for traditional light-scattering photoelectric detectors, while increased residence time near the heat source alters coagulation dynamics.
   - *Extinguishment & Cool Flames (FLEX, FLEX-2, CFI, ACME CFI-G):* Alkane fuel droplets can transition to quasi-steady low-temperature "cool flame" burning after apparent hot-flame extinction, presenting hidden reignition hazards. Suppressant behavior (CO2, He, SF6, N2) differs significantly from terrestrial ratings.
   - *Soot and Flame Radiation (SPICE, SLICE, ACME Flame Design, E-FIELD, s-Flame):* Microgravity isolates diffusion from buoyancy, revealing power-law soot smoke-point scalings and distinct radiative extinction boundaries.
