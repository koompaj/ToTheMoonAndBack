## Notes: Which Experiments Have Been Studied Least
1. **Least Studied (1 primary peer-reviewed flight paper):**
   - **ACME CFI-G (Cool Flames Investigation with Gases)**: Only 1 primary peer-reviewed paper using flight data has been published to date (Kim et al., *Proc. Combust. Inst.* 39:1647-1656, 2023). This is because CFI-G flight testing on the ISS was completed relatively recently (June 2021). The other paper in its repository record (Waddell et al., 2023) is a terrestrial stagnation-flow study rather than a flight data analysis.
2. **Second Least Studied (2 flight papers each):**
   - **Saffire-III**: Studied predominantly in combination with Saffire-I (Urban et al., 2019), with dedicated operational status reports on NTRS (Ruff & Urban, 2016). Far fewer dedicated material papers exist compared to Saffire-II.
   - **SPICE**: Two key publications report its 55 microgravity smoke-point flight measurements (Dotson et al., AIAA 2010-1105 and *Fire Safety Journal* 46:550-555, 2011). Subsequent publications primarily use SPICE as background for later experiments like SLICE and ACME CLD Flame.
   - **Saffire-I**: Primary findings published in Urban et al. (2019) and numerical modeling validation in Li et al. (2019).
3. **Moderately Studied (3 flight papers each):**
   - **SAME-R**: Dedicated publications focus on flight-to-ground comparisons (Meyer et al., AIAA 2013-3433), particle size/morphology (Urban et al., AIAA 2012-3536), and smoke detector performance evaluation (Meyer et al., *Fire Safety Journal* 98:74-81, 2018).
   - **CFI**: Key publications report initial discovery of quasi-steady droplet cool flames (Nayagam et al., 2012), cool-flame extinction boundaries (Nayagam et al., 2015), and n-dodecane droplet combustion across pressures (Dietrich et al., 2024).
4. **Most Extensively Studied (4+ flight papers):**
   - **BASS & BASS-II**: These ISS glovebox solid combustion campaigns have the highest publication density, including multiple papers in *Combustion and Flame*, *Proceedings of the Combustion Institute*, *Fire Safety Journal*, and official NASA Technical Memoranda (e.g., NASA/TM-20210011385).
