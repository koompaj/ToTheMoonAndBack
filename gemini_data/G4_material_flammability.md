## Summary of Findings for Task G4
1. **Scope and Gravity Level of NASA-STD-6001 Test 1:**
   - Officially defined in NASA-STD-6001B Section 7.1.1: Determines if a material self-extinguishes within 15 cm (6 inches) and does not transfer burning debris.
   - Conducted in standard normal Earth gravity (1g) in a stagnant (quiescent) environment.
2. **Material Flammability Performance:**
   - **Teflon (PTFE):** Non-flammable across all spacecraft and exploration atmospheres (LOI > 95%).
   - **Kapton:** High oxygen index (37%–42%), passes Test 1 in standard 30% O2 / 10.2 psia atmospheres.
   - **Nomex (HT90-40):** Passes at 10.2 psia / 30% O2, but fails in exploration atmospheres (8.2 psia / 34% O2); exhibits lower oxygen limit in 0g by up to 4% O2.
   - **PMMA:** Readily fails Test 1; normal-gravity blowoff limit is ~16% O2, but burns down to 13.6% O2 in microgravity (Negative Oxygen Margin of Safety of ~2.4% O2).
   - **Ultem 1000:** Self-extinguishing thermoplastic; burns in 0g at up to 4% lower O2 mole fraction than in 1g Test 1.
   - **Cotton:** Highly flammable in all habitability atmospheres (LOI 18%–21%).
   - **SIBAL (75% cotton / 25% fiberglass):** Low-speed microgravity extinction limit ~16%–17% O2; retains matrix integrity after cotton burns.
   - **Silicone:** Flammability is thickness-dependent (0.25 mm burns, 1.00 mm self-extinguishes in 1-g); combustion governed by SiO2 barrier formation.
