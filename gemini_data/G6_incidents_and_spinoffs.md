## Notes on Task G6 Research
1. **Apollo 1 Fire (1967):** Sourced from the official *Report of Apollo 204 Review Board* (NASA History Division). Key findings verified: 100% O2 atmosphere at 16.7 psia, electrical arc initiator, extensive non-metallic combustibles, inward-opening hatch unable to open due to internal pressure before rupture, and recommendations severely restricting combustible materials.
2. **Apollo 13 Oxygen Tank Failure (1970):** Sourced from *Report of Apollo 13 Review Board* (NTRS ID 19700076776 / NASA-TM-X-65270, chaired by Edgar Cortright). Key findings verified: April 13, 1970 at 55:54:53 GET; nominal CM cabin at 5 psia pure O2; combustion of Teflon wire insulation initiated by electrical arcs in fan motor wiring; detected by loud bang, telemetry drops, and master alarm; self-extinguished when tank burst in Service Module vacuum; recommendation to eliminate all electrical wiring from contact with oxygen.
3. **Mir Fire (1997):** Sourced from NASA History Division's 25-year retrospective (*25 Years Ago: Fire Aboard Space Station Mir*) and NTRS technical reports. Key findings verified: February 24, 1997 (late Feb 23 US/GMT); Kvant-1 module; solid-fuel oxygen generator (Vika candle) ignited; Earth-normal atmosphere (101.3 kPa / 14.7 psi, 21% O2); detected by crew seeing torch-like flame and heavy smoke blocking Soyuz evacuation route; extinguished using foam from three onboard fire extinguishers; heavily influenced ISS fire safety design.
4. **Artemis II Smoke Alarm (2026):** Sourced from NASA news releases for the postflight news conference at JSC (April 16, 2026), AIAA Aerospace America crew debrief, and Orion ECLSS / FDS technical reports on NTRS. Verified: occurred on translunar return (~80,000 miles from Earth); optical smoke detector triggered alarm and automated ventilation fan cutoff; verified as sensor false alarm with zero fire or damage; troubleshot with Mission Control.
5. **Earth Spinoffs:** Sourced from official NASA Spinoff publications (spinoff.nasa.gov):
   - Firefighter breathing apparatus: Composite Overwrapped Pressure Vessels (COPVs) from Space Shuttle/Apollo cut tank weight by up to 50%, reducing injuries.
   - Smoke detector: Brunswick Incipient Fire Detector from Space Shuttle orbiters discriminates combustion particles from dust to prevent false alarms in mines.
   - Protective clothing: Polybenzimidazole (PBI) developed for Apollo spacesuits and transferred via Project FIRES into non-melting, heat-stable turnout gear.
   - Fine water mist extinguisher: ISS zero-leak valve technology transferred to industrial/military systems; FWM replaces CO2 units because ISS filtering respirators do not scrub CO2.
